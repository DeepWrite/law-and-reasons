# Editorial Depth Policy

Law and Reasons is a review of legal philosophy and legal theory. Its default standard is not a newsletter summary, encyclopedia paraphrase, or technology-law digest. A publishable article should aim at the depth expected in leading Anglophone jurisprudence venues, while remaining usable for bilingual Korean-English publication.

## Core Standard

Every issue should be built from primary or near-primary research:

1. representative articles in leading legal philosophy and legal theory journals;
2. canonical monographs and articles by the major legal philosophers relevant to the issue;
3. historically responsible contextual reconstruction in the spirit of Gerald J. Postema's account of twentieth-century common-law legal philosophy;
4. SEP-level field mapping for orientation, but not as a substitute for primary analysis;
5. careful engagement with Jeyoun Son's work where it is directly relevant to the jurisprudential question.

## Topic Priority

The center of gravity is legal philosophy: Hart and post-Hart positivism, Razian authority and reasons, Dworkinian interpretivism, natural law theory, Fullerian legality, rights theory, rule of law, legal reasoning, German public law theory, European constitutional theory, discourse theory, systems theory, private law theory, responsibility, and legal pluralism.

Applied topics, including AI and computation, are admissible only when they are genuine jurisprudential pressure points. They should be included when leading legal philosophers or top-tier legal philosophy journals treat them as bearing on authority, legal content, intention, authorship, interpretation, responsibility, legality, rights, or institutional trust. They should not displace the core legal-philosophical frame.

## Source Discipline

Access levels and analysis levels must be stated. The magazine must not pretend to have read a work when only metadata, abstracts, tables of contents, or reviews are available. Claims requiring close textual engagement require verified source review. Book reviews and later historiography may help orient the field, but they must be marked as such and cannot replace direct engagement with the work under review.

Public source discipline is also privacy discipline. Public repository files should say only what is needed for the public record: source type, public verification status, analysis level, and whether further editorial verification is needed. They must not publish filesystem paths or working notes.

## Korean Translation Discipline

Korean translation is evaluated on two independent dimensions. Both must pass before `translation_status: checked` is recorded.

**Term discipline** is the primary dimension. The governing document is `editorial/korean_translation_policy.md`. Korean prose may be polished for readability, but it must not flatten source distinctions among reason/reasons/Reason, law/laws, source/foundation/ground/reference, obligation/duty/being obliged, validity/correctness/legitimacy/justification, Hartian acceptance, and interpretive-convergence uptake. Departures require a recorded reason.

**Prose quality** is the second dimension. The governing document is `editorial/style_guide_ko.md`. A Korean text that passes term-discipline review but reads as mechanical English calque has not completed translation review. The Korean version must read as natural Korean legal-philosophical scholarship — as if it had been written in Korean from the start. 번역투 (translation-ese) is a failure mode even when content and terms are correctly rendered.

## Historical Discipline

Historical issues must avoid presentism. Time-situated analysis, retrospective editor's notes, and archival uncertainty must remain visibly separate. Later canonical status is not evidence of contemporary centrality unless contemporary reception supports that claim.

## Historical Editorial-Agent Discipline

Historical model issues must not be maintained as generic table-of-contents scaffolds. Each public model issue must record section-level editorial responsibility in front matter and, where the issue itself is public, in a reader-facing editorial-agent register.

The minimum role map is: historical jurisprudence editor, source coverage editor, bibliography editor, debate map editor, legal philosophy editor, retrospective editor, teaching editor, privacy reviewer, and final managing editor. These roles may be combined for a small model issue, but the issue must still state which role is responsible for each public section.

The role map is a publication gate, not decoration. The source register belongs to source coverage and privacy review; the field map belongs to a time-situated historical editor; journal and book coverage belongs to source and bibliography review; debate and concept sections belong to legal-philosophy review; retrospective sections must be explicitly marked as retrospective; teaching notes must preserve jurisprudential distinctions rather than turning the issue into a generic syllabus.

## Publication Discipline

A model issue may be temporarily approved to demonstrate editorial direction. Permanent publication, Korean translation, and issue finalization require separate Chief Editor approval.
