Historical Method

How the back-issue program avoids presentism

The editorial method separates three registers:

  1. time-situated analysis: what could reasonably be seen from the target year
  2. retrospective editor’s notes: what later became important but was not yet visible
  3. archival uncertainty: what cannot be confidently reconstructed from available sources

Starting hypotheses are prompts for source mapping, not findings. Later canonical status must not be treated as evidence of contemporary importance unless contemporary reception can be shown.

Editorial Depth

The standing depth policy applies to contemporary and historical issues alike. Articles should be grounded in representative legal philosophy journals, canonical legal-philosophical literature, Postema-level historical context where relevant, and SEP-level field orientation. Applied subjects, including AI and computation, are included only when they function as genuine jurisprudential pressure points.
