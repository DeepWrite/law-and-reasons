# Historical Issue: 2025 Agenda

This is a reconstruction workspace. A temporary public model issue has been approved for demonstration, but the permanent 2025 issue, Korean translation, and final publication still require separate approval.

## Core Editorial Principle

Reconstruct legal philosophy and legal theory as they could reasonably have appeared in 2025. Keep three registers separate:

1. time-situated analysis: what could reasonably be seen from 2025
2. retrospective editor's notes: what later became important but was not yet visible
3. archival uncertainty: what cannot be confidently reconstructed from available sources

## Starting Hypotheses To Verify

These are prompts for source mapping, not findings.

- Hartian and post-Hartian legal positivism: rule of recognition, social sources, legal content, and internal point of view.
- Razian authority and reasons: exclusionary reasons, claims to authority, state power, and institutional trust.
- Dworkinian interpretivism: fit, justification, disagreement, content, and legal authorship.
- Rights and normative positions: Hohfeldian analysis, standing, remedies, and private law theory.
- Rule of law theory: Fuller, Raz, Waldron, Postema, legality, fidelity, and mutual accountability.
- European and German legal theory: Alexy, Habermas, Luhmann, proportionality, discourse, and systems theory.
- AI and computational legal reasoning only where the topic is visible as a jurisprudential pressure point in leading legal philosophy venues.
