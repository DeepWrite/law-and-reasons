# Teaching Note: 2025

## Course Use

Teach the issue as a bridge between canonical jurisprudence and the 2025 journal field. The course should not ask students whether AI changed everything. It should ask how legal authority, legal content, rights, standing, and rule-of-law theory respond when institutional forms are under stress.

## Suggested Seminar Questions

1. What remains of Hart's rule of recognition when legal content is perspectival, contested, or revised at the highest level?
2. Does Razian authority still explain law's claim to guide action under conditions of democratic distrust and administrative complexity?
3. Can Dworkinian fit and justification explain legal authorship and legal content without over-moralizing law?
4. Why did Hohfeldian normative positions remain useful for rights, remedies, and standing in 2025?
5. What do Alexy, Habermas, Luhmann, and Postema add that an exclusively Anglo-American analytic frame may miss?

## Reading Sequence

1. Hart, Raz, and the sources-authority problem.
2. Dworkin, legal content, and interpretation.
3. Hohfeld, rights, standing, remedies, and private law.
4. Fuller, Postema, Alexy, Habermas, Luhmann, and rule-of-law theory.
5. Computational legal text as a narrow test case for authorship, responsibility, and institutional uptake.
