# Retrospective Note from June 2026

From June 2026, it is tempting to redescribe 2025 as a generative-AI year. The model issue rejects that framing. AI mattered in 2025 only insofar as it exposed orthodox jurisprudential problems: legal authorship without intention, authoritative text without accountable agency, legal reasoning without institutional uptake, and rule-of-law form without fidelity.

The more stable reconstruction is this: 2025 legal philosophy remained organized by Hart, Raz, Dworkin, Fuller, Finnis, Postema, Hohfeld, Alexy, Habermas, and Luhmann, while using new topics to test old claims about authority, reasons, interpretation, rights, and legality.

Jeyoun Son's interpretive convergence theory is noted as a relevant Korean contribution to the same problem space. It should not be represented as central in Anglophone 2025 reception absent further evidence.
