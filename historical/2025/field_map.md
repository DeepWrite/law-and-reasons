# Field Map of 2025

## Method

This field map supports the temporary public model issue. It distinguishes canonical background from 2025 visibility. It does not claim complete 2025 source coverage.

## Central Then

- Hartian and post-Hartian legal positivism: rule of recognition, internal point of view, legal content, and social sources.
- Razian authority and reasons: exclusionary reasons, service conception, de jure authority, de facto authority, claims to rule.
- Dworkinian interpretivism as the continuing challenge of fit, justification, disagreement, and legal content.
- Rights theory, Hohfeldian normative positions, standing, remedies, and private law theory.
- Rule of law theory after Fuller, Raz, Waldron, and Postema, especially methodological deadlock and accountability.

## Emerging Then

- Computational legal text as a jurisprudential test case for authorship, intention, authority, and institutional responsibility.
- Renewed attention to highest legal rules, intergenerational legal subjection, and legal perspectivalism.
- Rights of nature, animal agency, disinformation, and mental capacity as generalist legal theory topics in OJLS.

## Marginal Or Not Yet Visible

- A full Anglophone reception of Jeyoun Son's interpretive convergence theory was not yet visible in 2025. It is included as a relevant Korean legal-philosophical comparator, not as an already central global text.
- Korean, German, French, Italian, Spanish, and other non-English 2025 legal theory journal coverage still requires deeper language-specific source mapping.

## Later Important But Not Clearly Visible Then

- Whether GenAI legal authorship would become a durable jurisprudential subfield was not yet clear in 2025. It was visible through a leading *Law and Philosophy* article, but should not be allowed to dominate the issue retrospectively.

## Present Readers May Overestimate

- The novelty of AI as if it displaced Hart, Raz, Dworkin, Fuller, Finnis, Alexy, Habermas, Luhmann, and Postema.
- The idea that 2025 legal philosophy had moved beyond the Hart-Dworkin problem. It had not; it had reformulated the problem through authority, content, standing, rules, and institutional uptake.

## Present Readers May Underestimate

- The continuing force of Razian practical reason in current authority debates.
- Hohfeldian analysis as infrastructure for rights, remedies, private law, and public law.
- Postema's rule-of-law account as a bridge between analytic jurisprudence and institutional accountability.
